# Swords & Sorcery

> **An OSR Fantasy Hack of *Lasers & Feelings*** — v1.1 (2020) by James Lennox-Gordon ([UnknownDungeon.itch.io](https://unknowndungeon.itch.io/swords-and-sorcery)) — CC BY-SA 4.0. Hack of John Harper's *Lasers & Feelings*. Layout template by Nathanaël Roux (barkalotdesigns.com); graphics by Freepik and Lorc.
> Source PDF: `ref/sword-and-sorcery.pdf` (single-page A4, landscape). Condensed below for implementation reference.

You are an adventuring party for the **Fissure's Breach Adventurers' Guild**. You are tasked with exploring the world to uncover lost treasures, solve mysteries, and defeat evil.

One player becomes the **Guild Manager (GM)** while the rest create player characters (PCs). The GM is responsible for sending the PCs out into the world on adventures to have fun, make money, and keep the kingdom safe.

---

## Create Adventurers

1. **Choose an ancestry** for your character: Human, Elf, Dwarf, Gnome, Orc, Catfolk, or make one up.
2. **Choose a background** for your character: Noble, Sage, Thief, Soldier, Tracker, or Entertainer.
3. **Determine your S&S number:** Roll 1d6 (reroll 1s and 6s) to get a number from **2 to 5**. If the GM allows it, you can choose instead.
   - A higher number means you're better at **SWORDS** (physicality, fighting, sneaking, intimidation).
   - A lower number means you're better at **SORCERY** (magic, knowledge, insight, persuasion).
4. **Derive your attributes:**
   - **Hit Points (HP):** maximum HP = **3 × S&S number**. When you take damage, reduce current HP by that amount. If you reach **0 HP** you are knocked unconscious and your allies have **one chance** to save you before you die.
   - **Spirit (SP):** maximum SP = **10 − (2 × S&S number)**. When you successfully cast a spell, reduce current SP by an amount equal to the spell's level. You regain **all HP and SP** during a night's rest.
   - **Weapon Damage (WD):** WD = **S&S number − 1**. When you successfully hit with a weapon, roll **WD d6s** and deal damage equal to the **highest** roll.
   - **Encumbrance (EN):** EN = **S&S number + 3**. You can carry and wear a number of items equal to this number.
5. **Give your character a cool fantasy adventurer name** — e.g. *Gunther the Brave* — and optionally draw a picture.

---

## Magic

- You know a number of spells equal to your **maximum SP**.
- Casting a spell of **level 1 or higher** requires a **SORCERY** roll. If you fail, something bad and magical happens.
- If a spell deals damage or heals HP, roll a number of **d6s equal to the spell's level** and pick the highest. Deal damage or heal HP equal to **the roll + (2 × spell level)**. This can be split between multiple targets that are close to each other.
- Continuous effects last for **one minute**.
- Level-0 spells require no roll and cost no SP.

## Rolling the Dice

When you do something which is possible but could fail, roll **1d6** to find out how it goes.

- Roll **+1d if you're prepared** and **+1d if you're trained**. The GM tells you how many dice to roll, based on your character and the situation (1–3d6).
- Roll and compare **each die** to your **S&S number**:
  - If you're using **SWORDS** (physical, fighting) you want to roll **under** your S&S number.
  - If you're using **SORCERY** (magic, knowledge) you want to roll **over** your S&S number.

### Outcomes

| Successes | Result |
|---|---|
| **0** | It goes wrong. The GM says how things get worse somehow. |
| **1** | You barely manage it. The GM inflicts a complication, harm, or cost. |
| **2** | You do it well. Good job! |
| **3** | Critical success! The GM tells you some extra effect you get. |

### Divine Intervention

If you roll your **S&S number exactly**, you have **Divine Intervention**. A deity or otherworldly being gives you a special insight into what's going on. Ask the GM a question and they will answer honestly. Your roll counts as a success, but you may change your action and roll again instead.

Good questions: *What are they really feeling? Who's behind this? How could I get them to do what I want? What should I lookout for? What's the best way to do this thing? What's really going on here?*

---

## Helping

If you want to help someone else who is rolling, say how you try to help and **make a roll**. If you succeed, give them **+1d**.

## Running the Game

- **The game is a conversation.** Play to find out what happens. Describe the scene, introduce a threat (monster, trap, hazard, etc.), and ask the players what they do. Resolve actions in a sensible order and ask for rolls when the situation is uncertain.
- **Player skill over character skill.** Players can't rely on character abilities to solve problems. Ask how they disarm a trap or avoid a hazard. If they want to sweet-talk a guard, have them roleplay it out. Let players use their own puzzle-solving skills: SORCERY is not intelligence, and a character with S&S 5 can be just as smart — and help solve problems — as one with S&S 2.
- **Only players roll dice.** If a monster attacks, ask the players what they are doing. If they fail, they take damage or are put in a worse situation.
- **Monsters and other combatants** have **HP** and deal **damage (DMG)**. Decide how much of both using the Monsters table as a guide. An enemy's spell is a threat by itself, and a trap deals the same damage as a monster.
