# The Triple-O: Player Character Emulator

Triple-O is a standalone, system-agnostic framework designed for solo roleplayers who want to step into the Game Master (GM) seat. Instead of emulating a world for your character, this tool dynamically emulates the unpredictable actions, behaviors, and decisions of a party of Player Characters (PCs).

### What It's For

* **Running Published Modules:** Act as the GM and guide a group of emulated characters through pre-written adventures to see how they fare.
* **Testing Creations:** Stress-test your world-building, dungeons, or homebrew campaigns against simulated player behavior.
* **Managing Sidekicks:** Emulate hirelings, allies, or secondary characters while you focus on a primary protagonist.

---

## 1. Setup & Character Traits

Choose your preferred RPG system and create your PCs. As you build them, note down their defining **Traits**—backgrounds, alignments, flaws, favored skills, fears, and instincts.

These Traits are the engine of Triple-O. Whenever a character needs to make a decision, you will use these aspects to inform their approach. If your system doesn't provide strong narrative traits, write down a short sentence snippet (e.g., *"Always ready to fight,"* or *"Suspicious of authority"*).

## 2. The Core Mechanic: The Triple-O

Whenever character action is required and the outcome is uncertain or the stakes are high, pause and define three possible courses of action based on the current situation and the character's Traits.

* **The Obvious (Roll 4, 5, 6):** The default, expected action. Given their Traits, what is the most predictable thing they would do?
* **The Option (Roll 2, 3):** A reasonable alternative. What is another tactical or narrative choice that still makes perfect sense?
* **The Odd (Roll 1):** The unexpected. What is a left-field, impulsive, or atypical action they might attempt?

**Double Down (Advantage/Disadvantage):** If a character's Traits make a certain outcome highly likely, roll 2d6 and pick the result that most favors their established behavior.

## 3. Resolving Questions & Group Actions

Use the same 1d6 framework to answer specific GM questions about character intent.

* *Example:* "Do they search for traps?"
* **Obvious (4-6):** Yes, they check thoroughly. **Option (2-3):** They check only the main path. **Odd (1):** They completely ignore the traps and rush in.

### Group Decisions

When the party faces a dilemma and must act together:

1. Select three different character Traits that apply to the situation.
2. Define three different plans of action based on those Traits.
3. Assign the most widely supported plan to **The Obvious**, the compromise to **The Option**, and the most controversial to **The Odd**.
4. Roll 1d6 to see what the party agrees upon.

## 4. Spark Tables

When you are stuck or need to interpret *how* a character executes an action, roll on these spark tables to generate spontaneous motivations and methods. Combine elements from multiple tables (e.g., Action + Method) to visualize the scene.

### Disposition & Motivation (Roll 1d6)

| Roll | Disposition | Motivation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Aggressive / Confrontational | Greed / Wealth |
| **2** | Cautious / Defensive | Survival / Safety |
| **3** | Inquisitive / Curious | Knowledge / Discovery |
| **4** | Helpful / Cooperative | Loyalty / Duty |
| **5** | Aloof / Dismissive | Pride / Glory |
| **6** | Erratic / Impulsive | Justice / Vengeance |

### Action & Method (Roll 1d6)

| Roll | Action Focus | Preferred Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Investigate the surroundings | Stealth / Subterfuge |
| **2** | Attack or confront the obstacle | Brute Force / Violence |
| **3** | Defend or fortify a position | Diplomacy / Charm |
| **4** | Negotiate or speak with NPCs | Agility / Speed |
| **5** | Manipulate an object or device | Magic / Technology |
| **6** | Flee, reposition, or hide | Deception / Trickery |
